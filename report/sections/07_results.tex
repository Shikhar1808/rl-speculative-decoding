\subsection{Measured costs}
On our RTX~3050 laptop a draft step costs $c=\numCqwen$ of a 5-token target verify for the Qwen pair
(\tDraftQwen{}\,ms vs \tTargetQwen{}\,ms) and $c=\numCsmol$ for SmolLM2 (\tDraftSmol{}\,ms vs \tTargetSmol{}\,ms).
Without CUDA graphs these were 0.81 and 1.44: a one-token step was limited by kernel-launch overhead on
Windows rather than by model size, so the small models were nearly as slow as (SmolLM2: slower than) the large
ones and speculation could not pay off. Recording each forward pass as a CUDA graph removed that overhead for
every method, including the target-alone baseline.

\subsection{Comparing the four algorithms}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Model-based vs model-free.} Value Iteration beats Q-learning on the same 80 states and trains
  in about a second. It plans with an exact count-based model of the logs, while Q-learning estimates the
  same values from noisy sampled rounds and needed 10\,000 episodes to come close
  (Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap}).
  \item \textbf{Tabular vs function approximation.} DQN beats Q-learning: it sees five continuous features,
  including the latest token's confidence $p$ and the entropy $H$, and generalises across similar states, at
  about 20 times the training cost (Table~\ref{tab:training}).
  \item \textbf{Value-based vs policy-based.} REINFORCE beats DQN on Qwen for every seed (1.649 vs 1.623
  mean); on SmolLM2 they are within seed noise (Table~\ref{tab:seeds}). Per-round episodes keep REINFORCE's
  variance low (its seed spread is the smallest on Qwen), and its training curve rises fastest (Figure~\ref{fig:curves}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{A bug the comparison exposed.} Q-learning first scored 1.28$\times$, below fixed $k=1$. Comparing
its table with Value Iteration's showed why: \CONT{} at $s=8$ is impossible, so its table entry was never
updated, and its initial value of 0 won the $\max$ in the bootstrap target, teaching the agent to always
continue. DQN had the same flaw. Excluding that action from the target fixed both.

\subsection{Correctness}
On 20 test prompts and four policies, 16--18 of 20 speculative outputs were identical to the target's own
greedy output. Every difference started at an exact near-tie of the target's top two tokens (logit gap
0.000--0.016), where fp16 rounding of a multi-token verify pass differs from one-token steps; none occurred
at a clear gap, so the acceptance and cache logic are correct.
